Origins in Taiwan

Milk tea, also known as bubble tea, was invented in Taiwan during the 1980s. Two tea houses—Chun Shui Tang in Taichung and Hanlin Tea Room in Tainan—both claim the creation, with Chun Shui Tang introducing cold tea inspired by Japanese iced coffee and Hanlin adding white tapioca pearls.

Name “bubble” from the frothy texture

Rapid Global Expansion

From its Taiwanese roots, milk tea spread across Asia and reached the United States in the 1990s. Social media, franchise chains like Chatime and Gong Cha, and DIY kits accelerated its popularity, turning it into a cultural icon for Gen‑Z and millennials.

Flavor & Innovation

Today's milk teas go beyond classic black tea. Fruit flavors now hold the largest segment, while alternative milks (almond, soy, oat) cater to health‑conscious consumers. New toppings like popping boba and cheese foam keep the menu fresh.

  • Fruit flavors: 38.3% of flavor segment (2025)
  • Black tea base: 45.6% of ingredient share
  • Cheese foam trend since 2021
